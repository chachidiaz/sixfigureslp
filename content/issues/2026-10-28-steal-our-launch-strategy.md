We just had our most successful launch of the year, and here’s how we did it.

## First, the context

2025 was a beautiful year, personally and professionally. Our daughter was born alongside Emily’s signature course, the AAC Assessment Hub. The first launch generated over $50,000 in revenue and more than paid for maternity leave. The second launch, just six months later, brought in around $23,000.

In October 2025, we knew we wouldn’t relaunch for another year, and here’s why.

## The launch ingredients

<aside class="callout" data-label="Every live launch needs three things">

1. A really good, no-brainer, take-my-money offer
2. A dialed-in email sequence and social content
3. Traffic and an audience

</aside>

Waiting a year to relaunch the AAC Hub let us rebuild number 3 and gave us time to re-evaluate number 1.

Our second launch felt like it didn’t meet our goals, but with a whole other ass human to raise, we just needed to hit pause and take some time.

## The live launch strategy

Live launching is essentially a three-step framework: evaluate the data you have, decide on your launch mechanism, and execute the vision.

### Step 1: The evaluation

To figure out how to position this launch, I went back and looked at a few things:

- **Past email and social content performance.** Did our content drive enough traffic and eyeballs to the sales page?
  - Instagram engagement rates and views
  - ManyChat runs and click-through rates
  - Email open rates and click-through rates
- **Sales page views.** How many unique visitors did the sales page get, and where did they come from? Did enough people actually see the offer?
- **Purchase conversion rate.** Of the people who saw the sales page, how many actually bought?

> If we just took the revenue numbers at face value, they wouldn’t tell me where we went wrong or how to make it better.

Based on the data from our October 2025 launch, we got the views, email opens were good, and click-through rates were good. <mark>But the purchase conversion rate was lower than I wanted</mark>, so I started there.

I looked at the offer:

1. **Was the offer truly worth $187?** Did the course give SLPs enough value to justify the higher price tag? We felt it did. So next…
2. **Did the sales page actually communicate the offer?** Did it answer objections, show what’s inside the course, and leave SLPs feeling like this was a no-brainer? <mark>This is where it fell short.</mark>

I knew we needed a new design and a much stronger case for the value of the offer itself. We also had data suggesting the $187 price point was too high, so we:

1. Lowered the price back to $147
2. Majorly beefed up the “what’s included” section of the sales page
3. Redesigned the page and added modern motion components for a branded, professional feel
4. Added the live cohort and the virtual AAC course as bonuses to make it a no-brainer

Great. With that done, the question became: how do we want to launch this?

### Step 2: Decide on a launch mechanism

I love live webinars and masterclasses. They create energy and novelty, build trust and value, and position the creator as an authority. They also prepare people to buy.

From our last launch, we knew the live webinar generated tons of interest (over 2,000 people), but fewer than 5% of the people who signed up actually purchased.

If we were going to repeat that, we needed a topic that <mark>naturally opened up a knowledge gap between the free content and the paid AAC Hub</mark>.

That’s where AAC Evals for Beginners came from. It was a topic where Emily could deliver real value (not just a sales pitch) and still leave people wanting more.

Over 2,000 people registered and fewer than 300 showed up live, but that was intentional.

<aside class="callout" data-label="Why we skip the show-up bonus">

I go back and forth on offering show-up bonuses to encourage live attendance. A large majority of our audience are parents, moms specifically, and we don’t want to pressure them to show up live if they can’t. It matters that the people who show up actually take in the content: focused and receptive, not distracted, multitasking, and anxiously waiting for the free bonus they were promised.

</aside>

### Step 3: Execute the vision

I outline when each email goes out and the specific topic it covers, and Emily writes the emails.

For this promotion, Emily created a new AAC lead magnet and promoted it three weeks before the launch, which reached an additional 1,000 email subscribers. We promoted the webinar roughly 10 days ahead of time (2,000 email subscribers).

The result was a roughly three-week pre-launch runway: <mark>a list full of people who had consumed free, valuable content, were positioned to buy, and were interested specifically in AAC evaluation.</mark>

On top of that, Emily uses her Instagram stories strategically to stay top of mind with her audience every day, and plans 3–4 in-feed conversion posts that speak directly to the buyer’s pain points and the transformation.

<mark>Our audience should see the offer anywhere from 3 to 10 times during the promotion window.</mark>

## The early results

This launch JUST ended, so we haven’t done a full debrief yet, but early results are positive.

Our October 2025 launch was for a $187 offer and ran for 12 days (Monday through the following Friday). That’s a long time and a lot of emails. We made roughly $23k.

This launch, the offer was $147 for 8 days, and we’ve made $26k.

That tells me the conversion rate was higher, so our hard work paid off. If we’d run the sale for the full 12 days (plenty of people will ask to come in), I imagine we’d hit $30k fairly easily.

So why don’t we? Well, we kind of will.

For our full list, the promotion is done. But we’ll email smaller segments with an extended deadline to encourage them to buy, and we’ll let in the SLPs who email us. That should get us pretty close to the $30k mark.

All in all, it’s a win for us! Another $50k launch would be fantastic, but there are so many reasons it’s hard to replicate those results. For now, the Hub won’t launch again for another year, and <mark>it’s on evergreen, generating consistent revenue every single day</mark>.
