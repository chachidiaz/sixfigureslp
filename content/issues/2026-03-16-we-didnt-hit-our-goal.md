**Current Obsession:** AI learning…soooo much to learn!

At the tail end of February, Emily and I launched her new workshop “When AAC Isn’t Working”.

Emily was stoked.

She’s always excited about her new trainings, but she was over the moon excited.

We planned for months. We listed-builded, branded the product, and of course, Emily created the content with love and care like she does everything.

When it came time to launch…

Something was off.

Our first day sales are usually really good but this year’s sale were awful. So I looked at the numbers I usually look at:

👉 Sales page views<br>
👉 Instagram link clicks<br>
👉 ManyChat runs

And then finally found the culprit.

❌ Email open rates

Our email open rates range from 50%-60% for launch emails and our email open rates were down to 23%, 16%, 20% and so on. They were literally less than half of what we normally experience.

So wtf?

It was obvious at this point that our emails were going directly into spam folders, but why? We do everything right…

✅ Have a long domain history of sending emails<br>
✅ Always have great open rates and engagement<br>
✅ Our emails are almost never marked as spam by our subscribers<br>
✅ We have an over 99% deliverability rate to email inboxes

So again…what the fu…?

We were able to get on a chat with Flodesk (which was very helpful) and here is what they said.

## Here’s what we learned.

In December of 2025, Google recently changed some of their privacy/spam filtering settings for gmail (which accounts for well over half of our list). They essentially made the email inbox into an algorithm that takes into account email engagement such as time viewing an email, click rates, reply rates, etc. Most importantly, they significantly reduced the time it takes for a domain to “cool”, which is basically the time period between emails.

We don’t send emails when we don’t have anything to say. We don’t want to spam our subscribers with fluff or “value based” emails if they truly aren’t worth their time. It’s important to us.

So the last time we sent an email was during Black Friday, essentially two months before we launched and because this was well over the <mark>new 4 week domain cooling period</mark>, Google decided that we were likely hacked and were sending spam and our emails went directly into spam folders for roughly 60% of our list 😒.

Now, I’ve heard of domain warming before, but never for an account that has over five years of reputation and sending. This is absurd.

## What this means for you.

Because of the new gmail rules (that we, of course, have to follow) here is what I now suggest to my clients and to you:

✅ Plan on sending emails to your list at least once a month consistently. Make this a newsletter (skip the fluff and make it worth people’s time to read), launch email, free resource.

✅ Give people a reason to engage. Make the newsletter a great read. Make the freebie valuable so people want to click and download. Recap what you did on social so people click. They have to engage so google sees that your domain is sending good emails.

✅ Focus on your subject lines. Your subscribers can’t engage unless they’re opening your emails. Make them want to click.

✅ Before launching, make sure your open rates are stellar. If you aren’t launching to at the bare minimum of 30%-50% open rates, you’ll need to re-warm your audience by sending emails to smaller segments and getting them to engage. It takes awhile but launching to crickets, isn’t great.

## It wasn’t all bad.

While we didn’t hit our goal of $20k, we only fell short by $5k in large part to Emily’s social channels. They outperformed previous launches and we were able to get some views to the sales page with a stellar conversion rate of 11% sales page views/purchases.

So, I guess it wasn’t all bad but definitely learned a lot in the process that we’re happy to share with our Insiders. Things are always changing.
