Why your practice website isn’t booking calls

Three fixes you can make this week, starting with the button nobody can find.

The Insider team · Oct 14, 2026 · 4 min read

You spent a weekend building your website. You picked the colors, wrote the About page, and uploaded the nice headshot. And yet the phone isn’t ringing.

Here’s the thing: most parents land on your site from their phone, in the car line, with about thirty seconds to decide. If they can’t tell what you do and how to reach you in that window, they move on to the next name on the list.

Fix 1: Put the button where eyes land

Your “Book a Call” button should be the first thing someone sees, not something they scroll to find. Put it at the top of the page, then repeat it after every section. One clear action, everywhere.

Fix 2: Say who you help in one line

“Pediatric speech therapy” is a category. “Helping late talkers in Austin find their words” is a promise. The more specific your headline, the faster the right family knows they’re in the right place.

Fix 3: Answer the money question

Parents want to know if you take insurance and roughly what it costs before they ever pick up the phone. A short FAQ saves you a dozen awkward calls and brings in families who are ready to start.

Your to-do list this week

  1. Move your booking button above the fold.
  2. Rewrite your headline to name who you help and where.
  3. Add three FAQs: insurance, pricing, and what the first visit looks like.

That’s it. Three small changes, one afternoon. Hit reply and tell us which one you tackled first — we read every response.
